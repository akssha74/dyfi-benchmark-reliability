Subject: Presubmission inquiry — engineering reliability benchmark for model comparison

Dear Editors,

We are preparing a manuscript entitled “Reliable comparison of severe
felt-intensity classifiers with versioned and dependent event data: a USGS DYFI
benchmark” and would appreciate guidance on its suitability for the
*Journal of Engineering and Applied Science* as a Research Article.

The engineering question is whether performance and model-superiority claims
remain supportable when catalogue version, dependent-event splitting, temporal
evaluation, paired uncertainty, and reconstruction are controlled. The
manuscript releases a qualification workflow in which a candidate must pass
schema, proxy, role, and temporal audits before holdout scoring and paired
comparison. The public resource is:

https://github.com/akssha74/dyfi-benchmark-reliability

The frozen M≥5 case study contains 7,520 source records, 2,362 eligible events,
and a 430-event temporal holdout. A worked qualification rejects one
temporal-proxy candidate, supports four admissible learned baselines over
no-skill, and leaves the two lowest-Brier models unresolved, thereby preventing
an inadmissible comparison and a false winner declaration.

The contribution appears aligned with the journal's scope in artificial and
machine intelligence, computational methods, modelling and simulation, seismic
evaluation, and engineering research methods, and with its published benchmark
and engineering model-comparison articles. Could you advise whether this
contribution identity is appropriate for submission?
